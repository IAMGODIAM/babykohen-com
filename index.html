KOHEN KARTIER WILEYONE YEAR OF LIGHT
HIS LIGHT — 01

One year
of mornings.

One year of his mother’s songs. One year of hands learning to hold, a laugh still new to the world. We do not gather here because of how he was taken — we gather because of what he was given: a name, a family, a light that belonged to him alone.

You do not grieve alone, and his family does not grieve alone. This page holds his light so anyone, anywhere, can sit with it — and the Coalition's family support page carries resources for families walking this same road.

THE ODE — 02

“Justice is what love looks like in public.”

DR. CORNEL WEST

He was one year old.
One year of mornings, of milk and warm blankets,
of a laugh still new to the world.

◆

Senatobia knows his name. Mississippi knows his name.
Now the whole world says it softly, the way you say
a prayer you refuse to end: Kohen. Kohen. Kohen.

◆

Grief is love with nowhere left to land.
So we build it a place — here —
and we keep the light on.

◆

This page is love, in public.

One year of light

Before the case,
there was Kohen.

There were small hands, bright eyes, family arms, and the ordinary miracle of a child becoming himself. We remember his life before we name the failure that ended it.

ORDER OF SERVICE — 03

No doors.
No hours.

This service is held here, in the open, whenever you arrive.

I.

The Gathering

Wherever you are — a kitchen, a porch, a parked car — you have arrived. Be still a moment.

II.

The Remembrance

Look on his portrait. Say his name aloud, once, gently. Names are how we carry one another.

III.

The Ode

Read slowly. Let the words keep him company. There is no hurry in this room.

IV.

The Candlelight

Light a candle below and let it burn where you are. It will be here, still lit, when you return.

V.

The Letters

Post a public word — your love for Kohen, or your anger at what took him. Both belong here.

VI.

The Benediction

Go and be tender with the living. Love loudly. That is the ode, continued.

THE BENEDICTION — 07

Go and be tender
with the living.

Kohen was one. Police violence killed him. It does not own the record of who he was. The record begins with his light, and every candle joins a growing movement of public witness.
